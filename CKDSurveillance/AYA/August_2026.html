Increasing Use of GLP-1 RA Medications Among Adults With Type 2 Diabetes and Chronic Kidney Disease

August 2026

Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) are increasingly used because they do more than lower glucose or support weight loss; these medications also improve cardiovascular, kidney, and weight-related outcomes in selected patients.1,2

Using data from 409,415 Military Health System (MHS) beneficiaries with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease (CKD) between 2016 and 2023, we examined the trend in use of GLP-1 RAs by CKD stage.

Overall, the use of GLP-1 RAs increased from 5.5% in 2016 to 36.6% in 2023, and people with CKD stages 1–4 had a similar increase throughout the study period. Among people with stage 5 CKD, GLP-1 RA use was 6.0% in 2020 and 20.4% in 2023.

The rapidly increasing use of GLP-1 RAs shows a change in how type 2 diabetes and CKD are managed, moving beyond a focus on lowering blood sugar alone, and reflects the growing evidence of their use in those with kidney disease, type 2 diabetes, obesity, and cardiovascular disease.
