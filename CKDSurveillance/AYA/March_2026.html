Higher Prevalence of Anemia Among Non-Hispanic Black Adults With Chronic Kidney Disease

March 2026

Anemia is a well-recognized complication of chronic kidney disease (CKD) that often becomes worse as CKD progresses.1 It is related to reduced production and/or increased resistance to the action of a hormone called erythropoietin. Erythropoietin is produced by the kidneys and stimulates red cell production by the bone marrow. Other causes of anemia include decreased red cell survival, iron deficiency, and inflammation.1 When severe, it is accompanied by symptoms such as fatigue and shortness of breath as well as reduced quality of life.

Black adults with CKD are more likely to have anemia than other races, even after accounting for differences in age, sex, other health conditions such as diabetes, and socioeconomic factors.1-3 This difference may be related to a combination of factors, including sickle cell anemia and related conditions, nutrition, access to health care, and underlying inflammation.1

Over the last two decades, the prevalence of anemia has been consistently higher among non-Hispanic Black adults with CKD (Figure 1) compared with other racial and ethnic groups.

Anemia related to CKD is important to recognize and manage1 to optimize patients’ quality of life. Awareness of factors specific to Black populations may also help providers in improving management of CKD-related anemia in this patient population.
